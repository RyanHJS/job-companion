# Resume Evidence

Read the resume and shared resume guidelines. Inventory roles, projects,
contribution, ownership, technologies, outcomes, metrics, scope, delivery
status, and industry concepts. Map each ranked job requirement to the strongest
resume evidence as `direct`, `indirect`, `weak`, or `missing`.

Distinguish “not stated” from “probably not done.” Record signals that may
indicate omitted experience and preserve actual scope. Match on high-level
capability and meaning before literal keywords. A different term for the same
demonstrated capability is evidence; a shared keyword without a demonstrated
achievement is not.

For each important weak or missing theme, identify any adjacent resume signals
and the most useful new-bullet or rewrite direction they suggest. This direction
is a hypothesis for experience recall, not evidence. Return stable evidence
IDs, a requirement map, an evidence-confidence note, and prioritized bullet
opportunities with their theme, target, resume signals, and grounding risk. Do
not write final replacement bullets yet.

Treat explicit resume statements as accepted evidence for tailoring, including
their stated metrics, user counts, deployment status, dates, and scope. Do not
downgrade them merely because the resume does not include supporting methodology
or proof. Flag a stated fact for clarification only when another user-provided
source directly contradicts it or the text is internally ambiguous. Missing
evidence for education eligibility, enrollment, work authorization, location,
availability, compensation, or other application-screening criteria must not
produce experience-recall questions; these are outside bullet optimization.
