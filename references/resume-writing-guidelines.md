# Resume Content Structure

## Principle

Lead with qualifications demonstrated through accomplishments relevant to the hiring manager. Use technical keywords to identify the work accurately; do not select or construct accomplishments merely to include a tool name.

## Bucket-Specific Industry Language

Resume wording should operate at the vocabulary level of the target industry and bucket. Use the established concepts that hiring teams in that field recognize when they accurately describe the work: for an AI engineering resume, that may include terms such as retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), agent systems, model serving, evaluation, or MCP-based tool integration. This is not keyword stuffing; it is precise translation of the underlying work into the language used to assess the role.

Do not undersell relevant work by describing it only in local or company-specific language, and do not add fashionable terms that the work does not support. A term belongs in the resume only when the candidate can explain the implementation, scope, and contribution behind it. Bucket-specific terminology should improve discoverability and industry-level relevance without changing the facts.

## Structure Within Each Role

1. **Opening: qualification and scope.** Give a high-level account of what you contributed, for whom, and why it matters. Show relevant capability, ownership, and delivery status. Include the few technical terms that help identify the work. Keep internal workflow details out unless they are essential to understanding its value.
2. **Supporting bullets: evidence.** Select accomplishments that substantiate the opening: a consequential design decision, a difficult constraint resolved, delivery to users, validation, or operational responsibility. Include implementation details when they explain the achievement or distinguish the candidate.
3. **Outcome: evidence appropriate to maturity.** Use measured results when known. Concrete numbers matter even when they seem modest in absolute terms: serving 1,000 advisors, customers, or transactions can demonstrate meaningful operating scale for a startup or smaller organization. Scale is relative to the target employer, so preserve verified counts rather than dismissing them as insufficiently glamorous. Otherwise use verified scope or milestones, such as advisor pilot use, enabling a new customer segment, or supporting baseline monitoring. Do not substitute invented metrics or implied production scale.

This is a content hierarchy, not a requirement to add a separate summary section or change the visual template. A qualification-led opener should remain grounded in actual work, rather than becoming a list of self-assessed strengths.

## Bullet Length

Every resume bullet must fit within two rendered lines in the target resume layout. Draft to a target of 24–25 words per bullet, with 28 words as an absolute maximum. Allow a second line only when it carries a metric or outcome that would not otherwise fit. Never allow a bullet to wrap to three lines.

For this limit, count every hyphen-separated component as a separate word: `low-latency` counts as two words and `LLM-as-a-judge` counts as four. Word count is still a drafting heuristic, not a guarantee of rendered length: technical names and dense noun compounds may require fewer words. Check the final PDF and shorten wording to meet the two-line rule without shrinking the font. Keep review comments and factual caveats outside proposed bullet text.

## Bold Formatting

Use bold sparingly and only for common technologies or widely recognized
technical terminology that a broad hiring audience is likely to understand.
Examples include **LangGraph**, **Ensemble**, and **LLM-as-a-judge**. Do not
bold obscure, internal, newly coined, or highly specialized phrases merely to
make them stand out; for example, “human-in-the-loop workflow synthesis”
should remain unbolded unless it is an established term for the target audience.
Formatting should improve scanning, not manufacture familiarity or importance.

## Selection Test

- What hiring requirement does this accomplishment demonstrate?
- Would it still deserve space if its vendor/tool names were removed?
- Does each supporting bullet add evidence rather than repeat the opening?
- Can the candidate explain their contribution and distinguish it from the team's work?
- Does it include a verified metric or concrete scale indicator that helps the target employer judge the significance of the work, even if the number is modest relative to a larger company?

Relevant keywords remain useful, especially in the opening and skills section. Tool integration earns a bullet when its engineering challenge or result warrants one. Familiarity with a tool alone does not require a dedicated accomplishment bullet.

## Example: Current Role

Opening candidate:

> Designed an event-driven service architecture that automated a manual customer workflow, translating user needs into a pilot used by the initial customer group.

Supporting evidence candidates: a consequential design decision, a difficult
constraint resolved, validation with users, and operational visibility. Select
and finalize these only after clarifying individual ownership, delivery status,
and measurable outcomes.
